Privacy Policy

Total Tactics · Last updated 3 November 2026

I’m Ilya Plotnikov, an independent developer. This policy explains what data Total Tactics handles and why. It’s an online game for Android and the web: your account and your battles live on my servers.

  • You sign in with your Google account. The game keeps your Google account number and your email address.
  • Your fighters, items, currency and battles are kept on my servers, so you can play on any device.
  • Battle server errors and suspected cheating are reported to Sentry.
  • On Android, you can choose to watch an ad from Google AdMob for a reward in the game. To show it, AdMob collects some device data.
  • If you agree, the game sends gameplay statistics to GameAnalytics. I don’t sell your data.

Your account

You sign in with Google Play Games on Android and with Sign in with Google in a browser. I never see your Google password. Google gives the game:

  • a number that identifies your Google account, so the same account opens on your phone and in your browser;
  • your email address, so an account you created earlier with that address can be recognised;
  • on Android, your Play Games player ID.

While the game is being tested, you may also be able to sign in with an email and a password. The game then stores your email and a hash of the password, never the password itself.

The game gives you a player name such as Player4821, and you can change it. Other players see it in battle results and leaderboards.

Game data

My servers keep what the game needs in order to work: your fighters, items and currency, a record of currency changes, and your battles and their results. The servers are rented from DigitalOcean in the United States, so your data is stored there wherever you play from. It is kept for as long as your account exists.

Data stored on your device

The game saves your sign-in token, settings and your answer to the “Data and privacy” question on your phone or in your browser. If you accept statistics, GameAnalytics also keeps a random number and any statistics not sent yet there. All of this is deleted when you uninstall the game or clear its data.

IP address

The server reads your IP address to limit repeated sign-in attempts. It is not saved in the game’s database or linked to your account.

Error and cheating reports

If the battle server hits an error or receives a suspicious command, it can send a report to Sentry, an error tracking service. A cheating report contains your account number, player name, game version and details of the battle. Sentry handles this data under its own privacy policy. I use these reports only to fix the game and to find cheaters.

Advertising

On Android, you can choose to watch a video ad from Google AdMob and get a reward in the game. There are no ads you didn’t ask for, and there are none in the browser. To show ads, count them and prevent fraud, Google AdMob may receive:

  • your device’s advertising ID;
  • device and app information, such as device model, Android version, language and game version;
  • your IP address and the approximate location derived from it;
  • information about the ads you see and tap.

Google handles this data under its own policy: how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

Your choices about ads

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the game asks for your consent to ads with Google’s consent form before the first ad. Where US state privacy laws apply, the same form lets you opt out of the sale or sharing of your data.
  • You can change your choice at any time in the game’s settings: Privacy. If you decline, you can still watch ads for a reward, but they won’t be based on your interests.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings: Settings → Privacy → Ads (the path depends on your device).

Statistics

The first time you open the game, it asks whether it may collect statistics. If you decline, nothing is sent. If you accept, the game sends them to GameAnalytics, an analytics service. You can change your answer at any time in the game’s settings: Privacy.

The statistics are:

  • a random number that identifies your device;
  • technical information about your device, such as its model and operating system, and the game version;
  • when you play and for how long;
  • how battles go: the mode, the map, whether the opponent was a bot, the result, how long you waited and how long the battle lasted;
  • the in-game money you earn and what you spend it on.

GameAnalytics uses your IP address to work out your approximate location (country, region and city), then keeps it only in a shortened form, for up to 30 days. The game doesn’t send your name, email, account number or advertising ID, and the statistics aren’t linked to your account. I use them to fix bugs and balance the game. GameAnalytics handles this data as described in its privacy notice.

Deleting your data

Write to ilya.plotnikov.games@gmail.com from the email address of your account, and I will delete the account and the data linked to it within 30 days. You can also ask me for a copy of your data or to correct it.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13. I don’t knowingly collect personal information from children.

Sharing and selling

I don’t sell your data. The only third-party services involved are Google (sign-in and AdMob ads), DigitalOcean (servers) and Sentry (error and cheating reports), as described above.

Security

The game talks to the servers over encrypted connections. Sign-in tokens are stored on the servers only as hashes.

Changes

If the game starts using other services or collecting other data, I will update this page and the date at the top first.
